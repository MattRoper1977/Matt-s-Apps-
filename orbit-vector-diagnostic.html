FLIGHT MANUAL

This is a planning game, not a reflex test. Set the dials, then run a deterministic simulation. The same settings always produce the same path.

Objective

Place the satellite inside the glowing receiver ring. Each sector changes gravity, atmosphere, wind, or payload behaviour. The dials show major marks but hide the exact value until you spend one of three scans.

Efficient play

After a miss, study the path and alter one variable at a time. In atmosphere, each completed dial adjustment costs one energy point. In vacuum, operational adjustment cost is 1 + floor(payload mass ÷ 4); mass still does not alter gravitational acceleration. Scans cost three. A precise solution with fewer launches earns more stars.

Access

Every control works with touch, mouse, and keyboard. Arrow keys adjust a focused slider. Press R for a free, panic-safe return to the sector baseline. The voice button reads status callouts aloud, and the icon button removes visible wording while retaining accessible names.

Kepler gravity lab

The always-available LAB sector replaces constant downward gravity with a central point-mass field. Acceleration follows a = GM/r²; payload mass remains a decoy because gravitational acceleration is independent of the satellite mass in this model.

LOCAL PLAY DATA

The app records launch settings, miss distance, time between a miss and the next launch, whether one or several dials changed between attempts, and the operational energy spent. A 100-run ±3% Monte Carlo fan is calculated locally in a Blob Worker and is not stored as learner evidence. This supports a teacher conversation about scale reading, force prediction, and variable isolation. It is not a medical, psychological, or ability diagnosis.

Data is stored only in this browser using localStorage. It is never transmitted. The teacher panel can also create an bit-packed AES-GCM QR hand-off for the standalone portfolio hub, or export a high-resolution science-art SVG.
