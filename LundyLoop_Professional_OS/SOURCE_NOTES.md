# LundyLoop Pro — Source and Accuracy Notes

**Research check date:** 15 August 2026

The suite is a practical prototype informed by the sources below. It does not claim endorsement by Professor Laura Lundy, Queen’s University Belfast, the United Nations, the European Union, the Information Commissioner’s Office or the Department for Education.

## 1. Lundy model

Queen’s University Belfast describes the Lundy model as a child-participation model based on four key concepts: **Space, Voice, Audience and Influence**. The EU Children’s Participation Platform likewise presents the model as a way to put children’s right to be heard into practice.

Sources:

- [Queen’s University Belfast — Enabling the meaningful participation of children and young people globally](https://www.qub.ac.uk/Research/case-studies/childrens-participation-lundy-model.html)
- [Queen’s University Belfast — Laura Lundy research profile](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/persons/laura-lundy/)
- [EU Children’s Participation Platform — The Lundy Model](https://eu-for-children.europa.eu/about/lundy-model_en)
- [EU Lundy model briefing](https://eu-for-children.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/The-Lundy-model-of-child-participation.pdf)

### Design implication

The prototype does not treat “voice” as enough. It requires an identifiable audience and an influence/feedback stage. It also avoids automatically classifying a case onto a ladder or producing a pupil participation score.

## 2. UNCRC Article 12

Article 12 protects the right of a child capable of forming views to express them freely in matters affecting them and to have those views given due weight according to age and maturity.

Sources:

- [OHCHR — Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://www.ohchr.org/en/instruments-mechanisms/instruments/convention-rights-child)
- [UN Committee on the Rights of the Child — General Comment No. 12](https://www2.ohchr.org/english/bodies/crc/docs/advanceversions/crc-c-gc-12.pdf)

### Design implication

“Influence” is not represented as a promise that every first choice will be accepted. A reasoned refusal, partial decision or alternative can still sit within an authentic process where the view was considered and a specific response came back.

The tool’s audit matrix reflects the General Comment’s participation-quality requirements: transparent and informative, voluntary, respectful, relevant, child-friendly, inclusive, supported by training, safe and risk-sensitive, and accountable.

## 3. Feedback and the 4F framework

Laura Lundy’s work on tokenism argues for feedback that is sufficiently **full**, appropriately **child-friendly**, **fast** and **followed-up**.

Sources:

- [Lundy — In defence of tokenism? Children’s right to participate in collective decision-making](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/files/148759327/Tokenismfin.pdf)
- [Queen’s Policy Engagement — How to do better in children’s participation](https://blogs.qub.ac.uk/qpol/how-to-do-better-in-childrens-participation/)

### Design implication

The Return Studio separates drafting from actual return. A decision is not marked complete merely because a document exists. The pupil then has a review route, including “not now”, disagreement and reopening.

## 4. Safeguarding information sharing

The ICO states that data-protection law permits information sharing where required to identify and safeguard children at risk and that it may be more harmful not to share information needed to protect a child.

Source:

- [ICO — A 10 step guide to sharing information to safeguard children](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/a-10-step-guide-to-sharing-information-to-safeguard-children/)

### Design implication

The application includes a safeguarding bypass. It explicitly tells staff not to wait for the ordinary participation workflow, allows a transfer to be marked and offers local redaction. It does not attempt to make the browser record the safeguarding system.

## 5. Data protection

ICO guidance emphasises fair, proportionate and lawful sharing, security, data minimisation and additional protection for children’s and special-category information.

Sources:

- [ICO — UK GDPR guidance and resources](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/)
- [ICO — Special category data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/special-category-data/)

### Design implication

The prototype defaults toward aliases, redacted analytics export, explicit retention dates, local deletion, privacy shield, encrypted portable backup where supported, and warnings that browser storage is not secure cloud case management.

## 6. Current England safeguarding-guidance status

As checked on 15 August 2026, the Department for Education page states that **Keeping Children Safe in Education 2025** remains in force until 31 August 2026 and that **KCSIE 2026** comes into force on 1 September 2026.

Source:

- [Department for Education — Keeping children safe in education](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/keeping-children-safe-in-education--2)

### Design implication

The application links to the current DfE landing page rather than embedding a fixed edition. Local settings must still follow the guidance and policy currently in force when the tool is used.

## 7. Deliberate accuracy constraints

The suite deliberately does **not** claim that:

- a browser audit chain makes records immutable;
- a closed loop proves that a pupil agreed with the outcome;
- a mathematical score can determine whether participation was authentic;
- audio capture is guaranteed to work offline on every managed device;
- local browser storage meets school information-security requirements;
- the application itself confers legal compliance;
- pupil voice overrides safeguarding, legal, safety or assessment-validity duties.
